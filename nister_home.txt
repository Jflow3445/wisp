# NISTER CONVERSION-FIRST HOMEPAGE, LANDING PAGE, ONBOARDING & MESSAGING SPECIFICATION

## Objective

Use this specification to rewrite and redesign the public Nister acquisition experience currently being built.

This is a **conversion, positioning and UX-copy layer** on top of the approved Nister Owner Platform architecture.

It must NOT alter the approved production boundaries.

In particular:

* Do not rebuild Get Online.
* Do not rebuild Manage Access.
* Do not modify the captive portal.
* Do not modify resident authentication.
* Do not modify resident payment behavior.
* Do not modify MikroTik/RADIUS behavior.
* Do not add owner accounting hooks to `purchase.php`.
* Do not change existing hotspot/API routes.
* Keep the lead-first onboarding architecture.
* Keep owner/admin pages private and non-indexed.

The purpose of this work is to make more qualified hostel/property owners understand Nister quickly and submit a property for assessment.

---

# 1. CONVERSION PRINCIPLES

Apply these principles throughout the public site.

## One dominant conversion goal

For property-owner marketing pages, the primary conversion is:

**Request a Property Assessment**

Do not give six equally prominent CTAs.

Secondary actions may exist, but visual hierarchy should clearly favour the primary conversion.

---

## Above-the-fold clarity

Within the first viewport, a hostel owner should understand:

1. What Nister does.
2. Why it matters.
3. Who it is for.
4. What to do next.

The visitor should NOT have to scroll to discover what Nister actually sells.

---

## Benefit before technology

Do not lead with:

* RADIUS
* MikroTik
* captive portals
* network management systems
* payment APIs
* routers
* software architecture

Lead with the outcome.

The customer does not want RADIUS.

The customer wants:

> Residents connected without personally managing the whole system.

---

## Keep copy scannable

Use:

* short paragraphs
* clear headings
* bullets where useful
* generous whitespace
* bold emphasis sparingly
* visual demonstrations

Do not create walls of text.

Every major section should communicate its purpose even if the visitor reads only:

* heading
* one supporting sentence
* CTA

---

## Specific CTAs

Never use generic buttons such as:

* Submit
* Next
* Click Here
* Learn More

when a more descriptive action is possible.

Prefer:

* **Assess My Property**
* **Request a Property Assessment**
* **See How Nister Works**
* **Explore My Options**
* **Verify My Number**

The CTA should tell the visitor what happens next.

# 2. WHO WE ARE SPEAKING TO

Primary public-site persona:

> A hostel/property owner or manager who wants residents to have reliable internet but does not want to become responsible for operating a complicated network.

They may already have:

* MTN fibre
* Telecel fibre
* another fibre provider
* wireless broadband
* another commercially appropriate connection

Or they may have no adequate network yet.

Their questions are practical:

> Will this work in my hostel?

> How much work will this create for me?

> Will residents actually get good access?

> Can I see what is happening?

> Can residents pay themselves?

> Can the service help pay for itself?

> Do I have to buy the equipment?

> What does Nister actually do?

Write for that person.

Not for a network engineer.

# 3. THE NISTER POSITIONING

Nister should occupy this category:

## **Managed Wi-Fi for hostels and student accommodation**

The category explanation:

> Nister helps properties set up and manage resident Wi-Fi, access, payments and reporting without requiring the property owner to operate all of the technology themselves.

# 13. FIRST SECTION AFTER HERO

Heading:

# **Getting internet into the building is the easy part.**

Supporting copy:

> Fibre, routers and access points can give a hostel internet.
>
> Running that internet well for dozens or hundreds of residents is where things become difficult.

Then present four short problems.

### Coverage

**Residents need Wi-Fi where they actually live.**

A connection at reception is not enough when residents spend their time in rooms across multiple floors or buildings.

### Access

**One shared password quickly becomes difficult to control.**

Nister gives residents structured access instead of relying on a password that can spread indefinitely.

### Payments

**Collecting internet money should not become another administrative job.**

Where residents pay individually, Nister connects eligible purchases with the resident access experience.

### Visibility

**Owners should know what is happening.**

Approved property owners get a clearer view of their property, transactions, network status and applicable revenue information.

# 30. HOMEPAGE VERSUS LANDING PAGES

This distinction is essential.

## Homepage

The homepage serves:

* owners discovering Nister
* returning visitors
* residents looking for Get Online
* residents looking for Manage Access
* search visitors
* company information

Therefore retain a restrained navigation.

## Dedicated campaign landing page

A page created for:

* Google Ads
* LinkedIn campaign
* hostel-owner outreach
* specific email campaign

should have ONE conversion objective.

Example:

```text
/lp/managed-hostel-wifi
```

or another campaign-specific route following existing routing conventions.

On such a page:

* remove the full marketing navigation
* keep Nister logo
* optionally retain a small existing-user utility link
* focus the entire page on the campaign promise
* use one primary CTA
* repeat that same CTA through the page

Do not send highly targeted ad traffic to a generic homepage when a dedicated landing page can provide stronger message match.

# 52. AEO ANSWER BLOCKS

Important public pages should contain short direct answers to likely owner questions.

Example:

## Can I use Nister if my hostel already has fibre?

> **Yes, potentially.** Nister does not necessarily need to replace a suitable existing internet connection. We can manage the resident Wi-Fi, access and payment layer around it.

Then expand.

---

## Can my hostel earn from Wi-Fi?

> **Yes, where the agreed arrangement allows residents to purchase access individually.** Eligible purchases can be attributed to the property and divided according to its commercial terms with Nister.

Then expand.

---

## Does Nister have to own the equipment?

> **No.** In the standard owner-funded model, the property can own the equipment while Nister manages the service around it. Selected properties may also be considered for Nister-funded deployments.

---

## Can Wi-Fi simply be included in hostel fees?

> **Yes.** A property can choose an arrangement where internet is provided as an accommodation amenity rather than purchased separately by residents.

# 57. DO NOT USE FALSE URGENCY

Do not implement:

* Only 3 installations left!
* Applications close tonight!
* Limited slots!

Nister's sales cycle requires trust more than manufactured urgency.

only when verifiable.

# 61. TEN-SECOND NISTER EXPLANATION

> **Nister helps hostel owners manage Wi-Fi for residents — including access, payments and reporting — while giving the property the option to recover costs or earn from the service.**

---

# 62. THIRTY-SECOND NISTER EXPLANATION

> **Getting an internet connection is only the first step. A hostel still has to make Wi-Fi work across the property, manage who gets access, handle payments and deal with the service day to day. Nister manages that layer. If residents pay separately, the hostel can also recover costs or earn a share under its agreement with Nister.**

---

# 63. SALES RESPONSE: “WE ALREADY HAVE MTN FIBRE”

> **That's a good starting point. Nister doesn't necessarily need to replace your provider. We help manage what happens after the connection reaches the property — resident Wi-Fi, access, payments and the systems around running the service.**

---

# 64. SALES RESPONSE: “WHY CAN'T WE JUST INSTALL ROUTERS?”

> **You can. Nister is useful if you don't want the job to stop at installing the routers. We help manage resident access, payments, packages, reporting and the ongoing service around the network.**

---

# 65. SALES RESPONSE: “CAN WE MAKE MONEY FROM IT?”

> **If you choose a resident-paid arrangement, residents can purchase their internet access through Nister and your property's share can be tracked under the commercial terms we agree with you. Some properties may use that to recover connectivity costs, while others may generate additional revenue.**

---

# 66. SALES RESPONSE: “WHAT PERCENTAGE DOES NISTER TAKE?”

> **There isn't one percentage that has to apply to every property. The commercial arrangement depends on how the deployment is funded and how the internet service is paid for. We agree those terms before deployment.**

Never publicly default this answer to 10%.

# 69. FINAL IMPLEMENTATION CONSTRAINT

This specification changes **copy, presentation, information hierarchy and conversion UX**.

It does not authorize changes to the existing operational Nister network platform.

Where conversion requirements conflict with production compatibility:

**preserve production compatibility.**

Build conversion improvements in:

* `nister.org`
* new public marketing routes
* the property-lead flow
* owner-facing presentation

rather than modifying Get Online, Manage Access, resident purchasing, captive pages, RADIUS or router infrastructure.

The finished product should make Nister look simpler to the customer precisely because the complexity remains behind the scenes.
