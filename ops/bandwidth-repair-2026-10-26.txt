Torrent handling and bursts
  The shared 256 kbps P2P queue trees and their port/L7 classification are now
  disabled persistently. Torrents use the same account limits as other traffic.
  Legacy quarantine/drop rules remain disabled; no two-hour punishment was added.
  Each account uses PCQ by source address for upload and destination address for
  download, with bounded per-device queues. Multiple torrent connections do not
  earn extra shares within that account. New paid WAN connections have per-device
  ceilings of 512 for TCP and 256 for UDP; existing connections are not kicked.

  Burst factor is 3 for every plan. A five-second local scheduler samples WAN
  traffic twice and checks two pings, packet loss and CPU. High latency (>50 ms),
  loss, CPU >=60%, probe errors or excessive referenced load disable bursts.
  Extra burst capacity is reserved once across eligible accounts; accounts with
  traffic get precedence over idle connected accounts. Allocation rotates every
  five minutes, using the correct nanosecond conversion for RouterOS timestamps.
  A 16-second average and 75%-of-plan threshold produce a short initial burst,
  then sustained traffic returns to the plan rate. Unchanged ticks preserve the
  averaging history. VPS sync checks native scheduler/script metadata and fails closed
  if it is disabled/missing/stale; it does not launch competing guard jobs.

  The administrator reports the download can reach 300 Mbps and upload capacity
  is unknown. qos_site.json therefore uses 300 Mbps only as a download reference,
  with live health/load checks; upload reference 0 keeps normal plan upload rates
  and disables upload bursts until capacity is known. No fixed WAN bandwidth cap
  or production aggregate parent shaper was installed. Short diagnostic results
  were not used as WAN caps. Radio/client performance can also limit a transfer.
