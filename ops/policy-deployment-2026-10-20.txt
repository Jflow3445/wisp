NISTER WISP subscriber policy deployment - 20 October 2026
Final policy validation: 21:50 UTC

Deployed outcome
- Two-device admission is enforced by an explicit FreeRADIUS accounting check
  and by the router's login hook. Username variants share one account identity.
- Daily, Weekly and Monthly plan caps remain 3/5, 5/8 and 10/10 Mbps respectively
  (upload/download), shared across an account's devices.
- A login immediately attaches the device to its account queue. If no queue
  exists yet, a shared 1/1 Mbps fallback applies until the next plan refresh.
- Missing plan metadata receives the same conservative cap instead of being
  skipped without shaping. A separate 1/1 Mbps subnet fallback covers otherwise
  unclassified addresses; AP management has its own 5/5 Mbps queue.
- AP management bypasses now require the existing MAC plus the specific
  management IP and HotSpot server. Management addresses may send DNS/NTP;
  other forwarding from those addresses to WAN is blocked.
- Identified BitTorrent/DHT traffic shares a 256 kbps upload queue and a
  separate 256 kbps download queue across the hotspot.
- The connection-count quarantine and legacy blanket P2P drops are disabled.
  Normal users are no longer blocked for two hours merely for opening more
  than 200 TCP connections. Previous quarantine entries have no active drop
  rule using them.
- Existing TTL=1 tethering protection and globally disabled IPv6 are retained.

Device admission validation
The original configured RADIUS limit did not reject a third device in a
controlled test. The new explicit authorization guard was therefore required;
aligning radgroupcheck values alone was insufficient.

Final controlled RADIUS results:
  No existing test devices:          Access-Accept
  One existing test device:          Access-Accept
  Two existing, different new MAC:   Access-Reject
  Two existing, reauth existing MAC: Access-Accept

The test used reserved unused local/international username variants and
temporary zero-byte accounting fixtures. The fixtures and test credentials
were removed; normal audit trail records were retained. No real subscriber
was deliberately disconnected or impersonated for these tests. This was a
server-side admission test, not an onsite three-phone association test.

Traffic classification validation
Controlled test packets matched classic BitTorrent ports, the plaintext
BitTorrent handshake, and DHT signatures. Both torrent queue directions
processed test packets. The two-direction loop test recorded 412 upload bytes
and 156 download bytes through the torrent queues and one plaintext TCP match;
the separate DHT test recorded one UDP payload match.

The diagnostic TLS transfer through the temporary loop did not complete, so
these results certify classification and queue traversal, not a saturated
end-client throughput measurement. Both configured maximum rates were read
back as 256000 bits per second. Temporary diagnostic NAT rules, routes,
addresses and address-list entries were all removed and their absence checked.

Boundaries and practical limits
Encrypted/tunneled torrents cannot reliably be identified by these packet
signatures. Their traffic remains subject to the subscriber's account cap.
TTL protection deters ordinary routed tethering; application proxies/tunnels
cannot be comprehensively prevented with this mechanism alone.

No production credentials were rotated. SSH configuration, authorized_keys,
SMTP, OAuth, NisterAI credentials and PostgreSQL were untouched. No server
reboot, package upgrade, payment-flow change, captive HTML upload or NetFlow
capture deletion was performed.
