NISTER WISP anti-abuse policy audit - 20 October 2026
Live observations: approximately 21:08-21:15 UTC

Verdict
The ordinary authenticated subscriber path has two-device settings, shared
account speed queues, and an IPv4 tethering deterrent. The full implementation
cannot be signed off as enforcing every stated rule. There is an infrastructure
bypass exposure, conflicting RADIUS session limits, and torrent rules that
combine incomplete detection with an overly broad whole-device quarantine.

This was a read-only production audit. No live router rules, RADIUS policies,
database records, credentials, timers, or services were changed. Evidence files
were written into a restricted audit directory. Subscriber names and MAC
addresses in this report are omitted or represented by hashes.

1. High priority: infrastructure exception bypasses subscriber controls

Two enabled HotSpot IP bindings are type=bypassed and match a MAC address only:
- TP-Link EAP225 management on ether3, normally 192.168.88.10.
- Downstream AP/router management on ether2, normally 192.168.88.2.

Neither binding has an address restriction. The second MAC also appears in the
HotSpot host table with 192.168.87.71, the router's own 192.168.88.1, and several
public source addresses. This is broader than an exception for AP management.

At 21:15:07 UTC, 192.168.87.71 had:
- Zero active HotSpot sessions.
- No HS_ACTIVE address-list entry.
- No nister-acct-* queue targeting it.
- 41 tracked public-destination connections, all with replies and source NAT.
- 37 connections to TCP/UDP port 443 and four to port 5228.
- Approximately 195 KB original and 1.19 MB reply bytes in those connections.

Connection counters establish that the tracked connections have carried traffic;
they do not identify a person or prove deliberate abuse. The MAC-only exception
and missing subscriber controls are confirmed configuration exposures.

The anti-sharing TTL rule and torrent inspection jump both match HS_ACTIVE.
The account shaper enumerates active HotSpot accounts. Infrastructure-bypassed
traffic therefore is outside these controls. A downstream device presenting the
exempt MAC can evade the normal account path.

Recommended repair: establish why the ether2 device presents multiple source
addresses, restrict infrastructure exceptions to the intended management
addresses, and separate management from subscriber forwarding. Preserve the AP
management and VPS Winbox paths. Revalidate clients on that AP before removing
any exception; simply deleting it could interrupt service.

2. Two-device limit: router setting correct, RADIUS policy inconsistent

Confirmed settings:
- Active/default/limited/nopaid HotSpot user profiles: shared-users=2.
- HS_ACTIVE, HS_LIMITED and HS_NOPAID RADIUS replies: Port-Limit := 2.
- All 100 existing per-user Simultaneous-Use rows: := 2.
- Application radius_simultaneous_use_limit() returns 2.
- FreeRADIUS default virtual server has an enabled session { sql } section.
- SQL simultaneous-use count includes local and 233 username variants, groups
  duplicate accounting records by device, and considers updates within 10 min.
- Interim accounting is configured every three minutes.

Conflict:
radgroupcheck still contains Simultaneous-Use := 1 for HS_ACTIVE, HS_LIMITED,
and HS_NOPAID. SQL group processing is enabled. These contradict the stated
two-device contract and should be aligned with the user rows and Port-Limit.
No claim is made that the second login always fails: several accounts already
had two active sessions. Effective third-device rejection was not exercised.

Observed ordinary subscriber sessions:
- First snapshot: 13 devices across nine normalized accounts, maximum two.
- Later correlated snapshot: 11 devices across eight accounts, maximum two.
- All current active account groups had an individual Simultaneous-Use row.
- The two usernames without a limit had only expiry/quota metadata and were
  not active-policy accounts at the check.

Today's accounting contained one account with a peak of three overlapping
device intervals, totaling 157 seconds above two. Both short overlaps ended
with Lost-Service records. This can reflect stale/offline session cleanup and
does not establish three devices actually transferring simultaneously.

Port-Limit is per username. RADIUS normalization/counting is important to keep
local-format and 233-format usernames within one account-wide device budget.
These limits govern authenticated HotSpot sessions, not the number of devices
that can associate with the Wi-Fi radio before authentication.
The SQL accounting freshness window and admission/accounting race mean a strict
at-every-instant guarantee needs a controlled third-device/alias login test.
No real subscriber was disconnected or impersonated for this audit.

3. Plan speed caps: correct for observed authenticated accounts, with gaps

Current active plans, Mbps (upload / download):
Daily_Unlimited:    3 / 5
Weekly_Unlimited:   5 / 8
Monthly_Unlimited: 10 / 10

These agree across location_plans, legacy RADIUS plan metadata, and live simple
queue max-limit values. One queue targets all IPs for an account, including
username variants. Two devices share the account's cap rather than receiving
the full cap independently. Burst limits are zero.

All 11 devices in the later correlated snapshot had the correct account queue
and HS_ACTIVE entry. Queues had traffic counters and some observed drops.
There was no active IPv4 FastTrack or FastPath bypass. The unlimited dynamic
HotSpot catch-all queue sits after the account queues. No throughput saturation
test was run against a subscriber.

Implementation weaknesses:
- The external queue synchronizer runs every 20 seconds with five-second timer
  accuracy. New device addresses can wait for the next successful run before
  joining their account queue.
- Active profiles have no fallback rate-limit. Missing/invalid rate metadata
  causes the synchronizer to log a warning and skip that account. A new active
  account could therefore remain unshaped on this path.
- Such warnings existed today, but the identified account was HS_LIMITED;
  those warnings alone are not evidence of an unlimited paid-user session.
- Infrastructure-bypassed traffic in item 1 is not included in account queues.

Recommended repair: enforce shaping at admission or otherwise provide a safe
fallback while retaining the shared per-account cap. Alert on uncovered active
addresses and missing rate metadata rather than treating a skipped run as a
healthy policy state.

4. Tethering: active deterrent, not an absolute prevention mechanism

The enabled NISTER ANTI-SHARE TTL1 rule sets IPv4 TTL to 1 in postrouting for
destinations in HS_ACTIVE leaving bridge. Its counter increased during the
audit, reaching approximately 38.3 GB and 33.1 million packets. This prevents
ordinary additional routed forwarding of the received packets.

IPv6 is globally disabled, so it does not currently provide an IPv6 escape
around this IPv4-only rule. Active users retain remembered-device login.

This does not establish that every form of sharing has stopped. Application
proxies or tunnel-based relaying can appear as traffic generated by the
authenticated endpoint. Bypassed infrastructure is also outside the TTL rule.
No controlled downstream tethering-device test was performed.

5. Torrent handling: not a hard bandwidth throttle

There is no torrent queue tree, no torrent-specific simple queue, and no torrent
packet marking. The implementation attempts to block/quarantine traffic:
- HS_ACTIVE source traffic jumps to torrent-block before active allow-all.
- Classic BitTorrent TCP/UDP destination ports are dropped.
- L7 BitTorrent and DHT matches add the source IP to TORRENT_QUARANTINE for 1d.
- More than 200 TCP connections per source address adds it for two hours.
- A quarantined IP's entire traffic through that chain is dropped.

Counters at the final observation:
- Classic TCP ports: 0 packets.
- Classic UDP ports: 0 packets.
- L7 BitTorrent: 0 packets.
- L7 DHT: 0 packets.
- Connection-count quarantine: 91 packets.
- Quarantine drop: 121,553 packets, approximately 45.3 MB.
- One IP, 192.168.87.59, remained quarantined from 19:50:09.

Problems:
- A connection-count threshold does not establish torrent use. Normal software
  can reach it and lose all forwarded internet access for two hours.
- The BitTorrent TCP L7 rule matches connection-state=new, so ordinary payload
  sent after the TCP handshake is outside that rule's inspection.
- Inspection is entered for client-source traffic only. MikroTik documents that
  L7 detection needs both directions. This further limits reliable detection.
- Legacy HG3 P2P rules are below active allow-all; they do not repair the active
  subscriber inspection gaps.
- Classic ports and plaintext patterns cannot reliably identify encrypted,
  arbitrary-port or tunneled torrents. Zero L7 hits does not prove no torrents.
- The quarantine is tied to an IP, not an account/session. Address reassignment
  can expose a different device to a remaining quarantine.

Recommended repair: replace the whole-device connection-count punishment with
a deliberate traffic shaping policy. Keep verified protocol controls narrowly
scoped, measure false positives, and retain aggregate account caps for traffic
that cannot be classified reliably. Validate classification with permitted
test traffic before claiming comprehensive torrent enforcement.
