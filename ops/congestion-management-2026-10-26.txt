NISTER shared-link congestion management - 26 October 2026 UTC

Production behavior
  Traffic now follows a shared HTB budget, an account queue, and a device queue.
  Account guarantees are proportional to the purchased plan and fit inside 90%
  of the shared budget, with additional space left for capped pre-auth traffic.
  Unused guarantees remain borrowable. Each account's two devices share its
  guarantee; each device uses FQ-CoDel to manage its individual connections.
  Multiple torrent connections do not earn extra account or device guarantees.

  The unchanged normal plan ceilings are download/upload:
    Daily Unlimited:   5/3 Mbps
    Weekly Unlimited:  8/5 Mbps
    Monthly Unlimited: 15/10 Mbps
  Data plans retain their individual rates too. Rates are ceilings subject to
  shared capacity, not simultaneous minimum-speed guarantees for every account.
  Normal plan max-limit values are still read from RADIUS/location metadata.
  The congestion controller changes shared capacity and account CIRs, never the
  purchased plan's max-limit. A plan downgrade clears the old CIR safely before
  the new allocation. Logout/account removal clears stale device queues.

  Existing two-device admission, remembered paid-device login, blocked-profile
  zero-duration cookies, anti-sharing TTL policy and peer-connection ceilings
  remain. The old shared 256 kbps P2P shapers and quarantine rules stay disabled.
  No customer logout or router reboot was requested for this deployment.

Adaptive capacity and bursts
  Probes originate on the MikroTik, to two independent Internet destinations.
  Three consecutive samples showing more than 25 ms of additional delay (or
  substantial loss) reduce the most-loaded direction(s). One remote endpoint
  failing, a single latency spike, or low traffic alone cannot collapse the
  budget. A busy CPU core prevents upward exploration; the local burst guard
  also disables bursts on CPU, delay, loss, stale-controller or probe failures.

  Bounded site probes measured approximately 41-80 Mbps HTTP download. A routed
  POST upload probe delivered about 4.2 Mbps; its tunnel path makes it a lower
  bound rather than a definitive ISP capacity measurement. The starting shared
  budget is 70 Mbps download / 5 Mbps upload. Operating bounds are 10-300 Mbps
  download and 2-50 Mbps upload. The administrator's 300 Mbps download reference
  is retained; the 50 Mbps upload bound is an exploration ceiling, not a measured
  or advertised link speed. These are shared adaptive budgets, not plan rates.

  After three healthy samples, recovery toward the starting budget is bounded
  to 20% plus 100 kbps per pass, including after an idle period. Above that
  starting budget, actual demand is required and growth is 5% plus 100 kbps per
  pass. This prevents a transient reduction from becoming a long-lived idle cap.
  State persists across runs; counters reset across prolonged downtime.
  Invalid/incomplete observations retain the last forwarding budget.

  Conditional three-times bursts use the current shared budget rather than the
  static peak estimate. The original 16-second averaging window is retained.
  The controller runs from nister-congestion-control.timer on the VPS; all packet
  forwarding, per-device FQ-CoDel and the five-second burst guard run locally.
  A missing/stale VPS heartbeat disables extra bursts, while normal shaping
  continues with the last budget. No WAN FastTrack bypass or IPv6 path was added.

Rule audit and overhead
  Disabled 16 active legacy TLS mark rules: four wrote wg-allow and twelve
  duplicate rules wrote preauth-ok. No filter, NAT, raw, queue or route rule
  consumed those connection marks. The separate WG_AUTO address-list learning
  has NAT consumers and remains, along with the canonical captive/payment garden.
  The already disabled torrent/L7 rules do not process packets; they were not
  presented as an active bottleneck. Required TTL, hotspot bridge firewall,
  accounting/NetFlow and management rules were preserved.

  The full live client count exposed extra CLI-read overhead during rollout.
  Reconciliation now snapshots queue properties and HotSpot session properties
  rather than repeatedly issuing individual property reads. Repeated unchanged
  device reconciliation took approximately 0.82-0.89 seconds and produced zero
  queue writes in the controlled check. Account sync completed in 12-14 seconds
  under its unchanged 25-second service timeout. Routine reconciliation does
  not move unchanged queues or reset their burst history.

Validation
  Full regression suite: 234 reported checks, including 14 congestion decision
  cases and seven deployment-rendering cases. Native burst guard: 14 cases.
  Native device lifecycle covers first/second device, logout and account removal.
  RouterOS parses, Bash/Python syntax, protected configuration hashes and Winbox
  checks passed. Test-only routes, rules, scripts, queue types and queues are
  removed from their reserved namespace after validation.

  An initial plain-flow queue experiment gave an eight-flow device an excessive
  share, so that design was rejected. With separate account/device guarantees,
  the final shared-account test delivered an eight-stream/single-stream byte
  ratio of 1.10. Small HTTP requests completed in 1.00 and 2.10 seconds while
  bulk transfers ran. These are isolated tunnel-routed tests, not measurements
  of the affected iPhone or proof of a 300 Mbps forwarding rate.

  Initial live-start validation failures triggered automatic restoration of the
  previous router/files. The RouterOS heartbeat return was corrected and the
  final controller completed multiple successful scheduled passes. Deployment
  rescue timers were removed after successful health/management validation;
  exact before-images and a manual rollback remain in the private evidence dir.

  RADIUS local health, live accounting, tunnel and Winbox were checked alongside
  the rollout. API, public Wi-Fi and payment host checks accompany the final
  private report. Protected credentials/configurations were hashed, not printed.
  ADOPT_UNKNOWN_RADIUS_CLIENTS=0 and the disabled Starlink adoption timer remain.
  Regulatory NetFlow capture/retention and unrelated services were not changed.

  Closing checks: overall WISP health and local RADIUS passed; 26 recent
  accounting records were observed, with the newest update one second old.
  Wi-Fi/payment pages returned HTTP 200. The protected status API returned its
  expected HTTP 400 for a trusted-origin request without a username (a request
  without an origin/token is correctly forbidden). Winbox passed. All ten
  deployed source/configuration hashes matched the local release. Both queue
  sync and congestion control reported success. The existing disk warning and
  unrelated edu.nister.org certbot failure remain; no NISTER unit was failed.

Limits and operations
  Router shaping cannot fix radio interference, weak signal or radio airtime
  unfairness. AP login credentials are still unavailable; radio settings remain
  unchanged under the owner's earlier instruction. The reported iPhone speed
  has not been correlated to a new direct device test, so no claim is made that
  its specific 3.1 Mbps result has been resolved.
